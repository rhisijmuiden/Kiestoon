    Inbound and outbound routes¶

    Routes decide where calls go: inbound routes send each of your phone numbers to a destination, outbound routes pick the trunk for numbers your users dial. Both are on Trunks & routes.

    Inbound routes¶

    1. Open Trunks & routes › Inbound routes and click Add inbound route.
    2. Phone number: your number as the provider sends it. 0201234567, 31201234567 and +31201234567 are treated as the same number. Leave it empty for a catch-all route: every number that no other route matches.
    3. Send to: any destination, for example a time condition for your main number, a queue for a support number, an extension for a direct number or a fax box for your fax number.
    4. Caller name prefix (optional): shown before the caller's name on the phone display, for example Support:, so the person answering knows which number was called.

    A typical setup

    Number Send to
    Main number Time condition Office hours › IVR menu › ring group / queue
    Support number Call queue Support
    Direct numbers The employee's extension
    Fax number Fax box
    Empty (catch-all) Reception ring group

    Calls from someone in the phone book show the contact's name.

    Route many numbers at once¶

    When your trunks have phone numbers, the numbers that do not have a route yet are listed above the routes. Tick the numbers (or Select all), click Route numbers, and choose one destination. Each number gets its own route, named after the name you give plus the number, so you can later send one of them somewhere else. When typing a phone number in a route, the trunk numbers are offered as suggestions.

    Outbound routes¶

    Outbound routes are tried from top to bottom; the first route with a matching dial pattern is used. Use the arrows to change the order.

    1. Open Outbound routes and click Add outbound route.
    2. Click a preset: Dutch numbers (0[1-9]XXXXXXXX) or International (00X.), or write your own patterns.
    3. Add one or more trunks. They are tried in this order; the next trunk is used when one fails or is busy.
    4. Caller ID (optional) overrides the trunk's caller ID for this route. An extension's own outbound caller ID and the number of its extension group win over it.
    5. Who can use this route (optional): pick extension groups to give them their own route, for example over their own trunk. Others skip the route and take the next one that matches. Default: everyone.

    Dial patterns¶

    Symbol Means
    X Any digit 0-9
    Z 1-9
    N 2-9
    [1-5] A digit in the range
    . One or more digits

    Each pattern has three parts: prefix (removed before dialing), pattern and prepend (added before dialing). Example: prefix 9, pattern 0XXXXXXXXX lets users dial 9 for an outside line.

    Who may call what¶

    Routes say how a number is dialed; the extension says whether it may be dialed. Each extension has an outbound class (internal only, national, international) and a switch for premium-rate numbers. On top of that the fraud protection limits concurrent outbound calls and sends alerts.

    Emergency calls¶

    112 and 911 always work, from every extension, without a route, and even when a trunk limit or the fraud lock is active. Under Settings › Emergency calls you set the caller ID for emergency calls (a number tied to your address) and the order in which trunks are tried.

    Mobile app¶

    The Kiestoon app for iPhone and Android takes an extension along on the road. It rings with the calls for the extension, also when the app is closed and the phone is locked, and shows new voicemail as a notification with the AI summary. It has the same screens as the Windows softphone: colleagues with live status, the phone book, queues, voicemail and call history.

    Availability

    The apps are published by RHIS as Kiestoon in the App Store (iOS 16 and later) and Google Play (Android 10 and later). Until the store release they are available as a test version (TestFlight and a Google Play test track); ask RHIS for an invitation.

    Switch it on¶

    Open Mobile app in the System menu and turn on Allow the mobile app. Nothing else is needed: the phone system talks to the push service of RHIS by itself.

    Setting What it does
    Allow the mobile app Employees can link the app under My devices. Turning it off stops all wake-up notifications; linked phones stay in the list
    Push service address Leave it empty for https://push.rhis.nl. Only change it when you publish your own build of the app (see Your own push service)
    Test connection Checks that this server reaches the push service and shows whether it delivers to iPhone, Android or both

    The table Linked phones shows every phone with its employee, when the app was last used and when the last notification went out. Unlink removes a phone (for example a lost one); the app stops ringing at once. A phone whose app was removed is unlinked by itself at the next notification.

    Link the app (employees)¶

    1. Install Kiestoon from the App Store or Google Play.
    2. In the portal, open My devices and press Link mobile app. A QR code appears.
    3. Scan it with the camera of the phone. The app opens, links itself to your extension and asks to allow notifications and the microphone. Allow both: without notifications the app cannot ring when it is closed.

    The code works once and for 10 minutes. On a phone without a camera, open the app, choose Link with code and type the server address and the code. Linking uses the sign-in of the portal, so it works the same with a password, MFA, a passkey or single sign-on: the app itself never sees a password.

    Under My devices you see your linked phones. Send test notification checks that notifications arrive; Unlink removes a phone.

    Using the app¶

    The app has the screens of the Windows softphone, with the menu as a tab bar at the bottom. An incoming call shows on the phone's own call screen: on iPhone the CallKit screen (also on the lock screen and in the car), on Android a full-screen call with Answer and Decline. During a call the phone keeps the microphone and the audio running when you switch apps or lock the screen; on Android a notification shows the call in progress with Hang up.

    New voicemail shows as a notification. On Android it shows the caller and the AI summary; on iPhone it reads New voicemail, open the app to see who called and the summary.

    When Allow the mobile app is off, or the app was built without push (see Build the app), the app still works as a softphone while it is open, like the browser softphone.

    How a call reaches the phone¶

    A phone in a pocket has no open connection to the phone system: iOS and Android close it to save the battery. So the phone system wakes the app first:

    sequenceDiagram
    participant C as Caller
    participant K as Kiestoon
    participant R as Push service (RHIS)
    participant A as Apple / Google
    participant P as Phone
    C->>K: call for 204
    K->>R: wake 204 (signed, content encrypted)
    R->>A: VoIP push / high priority message
    A->>P: wake the app
    P->>P: shows the call (CallKit / Android call screen)
    P->>K: connects and registers (WebSocket, SRTP)
    K->>P: the waiting call rings

    The desk phone, the browser and the Windows softphone ring at the same moment, as always. The call waits up to 15 seconds for the phone to connect; on a normal 4G or Wi-Fi connection that takes one or two seconds. When the caller hangs up first, or a colleague answers, the call screen on the phone goes away again.

    Calls through ring groups and queues reach the app the same way. Intercom calls do not: the app never answers by itself.

    Privacy and security¶

    Apple and Google only deliver notifications for an app on behalf of its publisher, so a phone system cannot send them directly. The push service of RHIS forwards them and is built so that it needs as little trust as possible:

    • It stores nothing. When a phone links, the push service turns the push token of the phone into a sealed handle that only the push service can open, and forgets it. Your phone system keeps the handle.
    • It cannot read the notifications. Who is calling and the voicemail summary are encrypted with a key that only your phone system and the phone know. The push service only sees that a notification goes to a phone.
    • Every phone system has its own key (shown on the settings page). A handle only works with the key of the phone system that made it, and every request is signed and works once, so nobody else can ring your phones.
    • It logs no numbers, tokens or contents. The push service is open source (cmd/kiestoon-push in the repository).

    Calls themselves never go through RHIS: the app connects straight to your phone system with encrypted audio (WebRTC, DTLS-SRTP), the same way as the Windows softphone. For calls from outside the office the portal must be reachable from the internet, see Internet access and SSL.

    Your own push service¶

    Only the publisher of an app can send to it. When you build and publish the app yourself (under your own name in the stores), run your own push service with your own Apple and Google keys:

    1. Install the package kiestoon-push_<version>_amd64.deb (or arm64) from the releases on a small Debian server.
    2. Apple: create a key with Apple Push Notifications service under developer.apple.com > Keys, and copy the .p8 file to /etc/kiestoon-push/apns.p8. Google: create a service account key in the Firebase project and copy it to /etc/kiestoon-push/fcm.json. Give both chgrp kiestoon-push and chmod 0640.
    3. Fill in /etc/kiestoon-push/kiestoon-push.env (key id, team id, bundle id of your app), remove KIESTOON_PUSH_LOG_ONLY=1 and run systemctl restart kiestoon-push.
    4. Put an HTTPS proxy in front, for example Caddy: push.example.com { reverse_proxy 127.0.0.1:8095 }.
    5. On every phone system, enter https://push.example.com as Push service address and press Test connection.

    The secret in /var/lib/kiestoon-push/secret seals the handles. Back it up: with a new secret every phone has to open the app once to register again.

    Build the app¶

    The source of the app is in the folder mobile/ of the repository (Tauri 2, with a small native part in Kotlin and Swift for push and the call screen). Every change is built by GitHub Actions (workflow Mobile app): a debug APK for Android and a simulator build for iPhone. For your own store build you need:

    • Android: a Firebase project with an Android app nl.rhis.kiestoon (or your own id). Put its google-services.json in mobile/src-tauri/gen/android/app/; without it the app builds without push. Sign the release APK or bundle with your upload key.
    • iPhone: an Apple developer account, a Mac with Xcode, and the capabilities Push Notifications and Background Modes (Voice over IP, Audio, Remote notifications). Run npx tauri ios init and then ./ios-setup.sh in mobile/, which adds the background modes, the microphone text and the push entitlement.

    Troubleshooting¶

    Problem Solution
    The app only rings when it is open Notifications are not allowed for Kiestoon. Turn them on in the settings of the phone, open the app once, and use Send test notification
    Test connection says the push service cannot be reached The server needs outgoing HTTPS to push.rhis.nl (port 443). Check the firewall between the server and the internet
    The call screen appears but the call does not connect The phone cannot reach the portal over the internet. Check Internet access, or connect to the office Wi-Fi
    A phone no longer shows in the list The app was removed or reinstalled; Apple or Google then report the phone as gone and it is unlinked. Link it again
